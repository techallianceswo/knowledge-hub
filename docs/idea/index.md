# Idea stage

## Explore your idea and understand its potential

Have an idea for a new business, product, or service? Before investing significant time and resources, it's worth exploring the problem you want to solve, who experiences it, and why your proposed solution might matter.

The Idea stage helps you take a closer look at your opportunity, challenge assumptions, and build a clearer understanding of what you're working toward.

You don't need to have all the answers yet. This is your opportunity to research, ask questions, and explore what's possible.

## What you'll explore

The resources in this section can help you:

- **Define the problem:** Understand the challenge your idea aims to address and why it matters.
- **Identify potential customers:** Explore who might benefit from your solution and what their needs may be.
- **Research your market:** Learn about your industry, existing solutions, and competitors.
- **Clarify your value proposition:** Consider what makes your idea relevant to potential customers.
- **Identify your assumptions:** Recognize what you know, what you believe, and what you still need to investigate.

## How to use these resources

Explore the topics available in this section to develop your understanding of the opportunity you're considering.

Each topic may include practical notes, tools, templates, or links to additional reading that can help you explore different aspects of your idea.

If your TechAlliance advisor has recommended specific resources, start there. You can return to other topics as your questions and priorities evolve.

## Questions to consider

As you explore your idea, think about:

- What problem am I trying to solve?
- Who experiences this problem?
- How are potential customers addressing it today?
- What makes my proposed solution worth exploring?
- What do I still need to learn or validate?

Use these questions to guide your research, identify gaps in your understanding, and prepare for conversations with your TechAlliance advisor.

## Your next step

Start with the topic that addresses your most important question right now.

Use what you learn to refine your thinking, identify assumptions, and prepare to explore your opportunity further in the Discovery stage.
