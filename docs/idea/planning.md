# Product & Business Plan

## Is It Worth It?

Even though you consider your business still only in your head and you have no clients, investors, or employees yet, planning ahead is vital to making it more likely you'll have smooth sailing.

By planning, you understand where to focus, organize your priorities, or seek the help you might need to bring this idea to life. By planning, you can confidently seek resources and plan your specific steps to validate and deploy what you have in mind.

It's for this reason that institutions like banks will demand business plans to consider lending you money -they need to understand what you want to reach and which steps you're taking to do so.

Additionally, a proper strategic plan will guide every single decision in your organization, from the processes you will run to what profile you need for each role -the machines two factories will buy will heavily diverge if one seeks to offer the cheapest option in the market and the second wants to offer the highest quality goods its customers have ever purchased.

However, traditional business plans aren't the only way to create the basic plan a growing business needs, and for innovative tech startups, they're not even the most advisable option. Since Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur published their suggested methodology, the [Business Model Canvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_model_canvas), the planning stage for tech startups has been significantly streamlined, allowing them to accelerate their early stages while maintaining the core steps for a solid foundation.

And even though this initial plan can (and will) change as the idea/business evolves, this early definition works as a baseline that will help you define which potential customers you should interview, what you plan to offer, and how you plan to earn money.

## Business Tools

### [Business Model Canvas (BMC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_model_canvas)

Created by Alexander Osterwalder in 2004 and published in the 2010 book *Business Model Generation*, it served as one of the cornerstone concepts of the lean business era.

This tool allows business founders to plan a startup on a single sheet of paper, making the process easier and less bureaucratic:

![The BMC board](/knowledge-hub/assets/images/bmc.jpg)

### [Lean Canvas](https://ashmaurya.com/blog/what-is-lean-canvas)

Based on the BMC (above), the Lean Canvas was created by Ash Maurya and uses a slightly different approach: it focuses less on the company and more on potential customers and the product to be built.

Similarly to the BMC, it will allow founders to run strategic planning runs and consequent interactions in a more efficient manner:

![The Lean Canvas board](/knowledge-hub/assets/images/lean-canvas.png)

## Tech Support

### [Canvanizer](https://canvanizer.com/)

Free tool that lets users create and export various types of canvases, including BMC and Lean Canvas. Highly recognized in the startup and tech world.

### [Miro](https://miro.com/)

Whiteboard tool with a pretty generous free tier and a plethora of templates to choose from and work for your projects, including BMC, Lean Canvas, and Project Premortem.
