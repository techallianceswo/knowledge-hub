# Validation stage

## Test your solution and build confidence in your next steps

You've explored your idea and learned more about your potential customers. Now it's time to test whether your proposed solution addresses a real need and whether customers see enough value in it to take action.

The Validation stage helps you move from assumptions to evidence by testing your ideas, gathering feedback, and understanding what works, what needs improvement, and what may need to change.

You don't need a fully developed product to get started. This is your opportunity to experiment, learn from real customer interactions, and make more informed decisions about your next steps.

## What you'll explore

The resources in this section can help you:

- **Test your assumptions:** Identify the key beliefs behind your business idea and explore ways to test them.
- **Develop and test your solution:** Learn how prototypes, minimum viable products (MVPs), and early experiments can help you evaluate your approach.
- **Gather customer feedback:** Understand how potential customers respond to your solution and what their experiences can tell you.
- **Evaluate customer interest:** Explore ways to assess demand, willingness to pay, and early signs of customer traction.
- **Refine your approach:** Use the evidence you collect to improve your solution, revisit assumptions, or consider a different direction.

## How to use these resources

Explore the topics available in this section to understand different approaches to testing your solution and evaluating its potential.

Each topic may include practical notes, tools, templates, or links to additional reading that can help you design experiments, collect feedback, and assess your findings.

If your TechAlliance advisor has recommended specific resources, start there. You can return to other topics as your questions and priorities evolve.

## Questions to consider

As you test your solution, think about:

- What assumptions do I need to test before moving forward?
- What's the simplest way to test whether my solution addresses a real customer need?
- How are potential customers responding to my solution?
- What evidence do I have that customers are interested or willing to pay?
- What have I learned from my experiments and customer feedback?
- What should I improve, change, or investigate further?

Use these questions to evaluate your progress, identify gaps in your evidence, and prepare for conversations with your TechAlliance advisor.
