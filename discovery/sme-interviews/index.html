    Subject-matter expert (SME) interviews

    Learn from people who understand your industry

    When exploring a business idea, you don't need to figure everything out on your own.

    People with experience in your industry, technology, or target market can help you understand challenges, identify important considerations, and explore questions you may not have thought to ask.

    A subject-matter expert (SME) is someone with specialized knowledge or practical experience in a particular field.

    This could be an industry professional, technical specialist, researcher, regulatory advisor, experienced entrepreneur, or someone who has worked extensively with the type of problem you're investigating.

    Interviewing these experts can help you develop a better understanding of the environment in which your business might operate.

    SME interviews are optional, but highly recommended. They can provide valuable context alongside your customer discovery research, particularly when you're entering an unfamiliar, technical, or regulated market.

    Why speak with subject-matter experts?

    Customer discovery helps you understand how potential customers experience a problem.

    SME interviews help you understand the broader context surrounding that problem.

    For example, if you're developing a solution for healthcare organizations, conversations with healthcare professionals, technology specialists, and people familiar with relevant regulations may reveal considerations that aren't immediately obvious from customer interviews alone.

    SME interviews can help you:

    • Understand your industry: Learn how the market operates, who participates in it, and what influences decisions.
    • Explore technical considerations: Identify potential requirements, limitations, or challenges related to your proposed approach.
    • Understand regulations and standards: Learn which rules, certifications, or industry practices may be relevant to your business.
    • Identify existing solutions: Discover established approaches, emerging technologies, or alternatives you haven't considered.
    • Challenge assumptions: Hear informed perspectives that may differ from your initial understanding.
    • Identify opportunities: Explore gaps, changes, or emerging needs that may deserve further investigation.
    • Build connections: Meet people who may introduce you to additional experts, industry contacts, or potential customers.

    You don't need to become an expert in every area of your business.

    The goal is to learn enough to make better decisions, recognize important risks, and identify where additional research or specialized advice may be needed.

    SME interviews are not a replacement for customer discovery

    An industry expert may understand a market extremely well without experiencing the same challenges as your target customers.

    They may also have opinions about what customers need that don't fully reflect how those customers behave.

    For this reason, it's important to distinguish between:

    • Customer evidence: What potential customers say about their own experiences, supported by specific examples and behaviours.
    • Expert insight: What knowledgeable professionals explain about industry practices, trends, constraints, and possible opportunities.

    Both can inform your understanding, but they answer different questions.

    For example, an expert might explain why a particular process is inefficient across an industry.

    Customer interviews can help you understand whether the people performing that process recognize the inefficiency as a significant problem and what they're doing about it.

    Use expert insights to inform your research, not to replace direct evidence from potential customers.

    Who should you interview?

    The most relevant experts will depend on your business idea and the questions you're trying to answer.

    Consider speaking with:

    • Industry professionals: People with experience working in the market you want to enter.
    • Technical specialists: Professionals who understand the technologies, systems, or processes involved in your idea.
    • Researchers and academics: People studying relevant scientific, technical, economic, or social topics.
    • Regulatory and compliance specialists: Professionals familiar with applicable laws, standards, or industry requirements.
    • Experienced entrepreneurs: Founders or business leaders who have worked in similar markets.
    • Supply chain and distribution specialists: People who understand how products and services reach customers.
    • Industry advisors and consultants: Professionals who work with organizations facing challenges similar to those you're investigating.

    You may find relevant experts through professional associations, universities, research institutions, industry events, LinkedIn, and introductions from your existing network.

    Consider asking your TechAlliance advisor whether they can help you identify relevant expertise or connections.

    Start with the knowledge gaps that matter most to your business rather than trying to interview every possible type of expert.

    How to conduct an SME interview

    An effective SME interview requires preparation, curiosity, and a willingness to explore unexpected insights.

    1. Define what you want to learn

    Before contacting an expert, identify the questions you hope they can help answer.

    For example:

    • What are the main challenges facing this industry?
    • How is a particular process typically managed?
    • What technical or regulatory requirements should I understand?
    • What common mistakes do new businesses make in this market?

    Choose experts whose experience is relevant to those questions.

    2. Research the expert and the topic

    Learn about the expert's background, professional experience, and area of specialization.

    Review relevant industry information before the conversation so you can move beyond basic questions.

    You don't need to know everything, but preparation helps you make better use of the expert's time.

    3. Prepare thoughtful questions

    Develop an interview guide with open-ended questions.

    Avoid asking only for general opinions about your business idea.

    Instead, encourage experts to share specific examples, explain industry practices, and describe challenges they've encountered.

    4. Explain the purpose of the conversation

    Briefly introduce your venture and explain what you're trying to learn.

    Make it clear that you're seeking their perspective and experience.

    Be respectful of their time, particularly when someone is sharing specialized knowledge without compensation.

    If you want to record the conversation, ask for permission first.

    5. Follow interesting insights

    Use your prepared questions as a guide, but don't be afraid to explore unexpected topics.

    If an expert mentions an unfamiliar challenge or surprising trend, ask them to explain further.

    Questions such as "Can you give me an example?" or "Why does that happen?" can help you move beyond general observations.

    6. Summarize and clarify

    Before ending the interview, briefly summarize what you've learned and check that you've understood the expert correctly.

    Ask whether there are important considerations you haven't discussed.

    You can also ask whether they recommend additional resources or other people who might offer a useful perspective.

    7. Document your findings

    Review your notes soon after the conversation.

    Record important insights, examples, recommended resources, and questions that need further investigation.

    Distinguish between established facts, the expert's professional opinions, and your own interpretations.

    Business tools

    SME interview guide

    Use these questions as a starting point when preparing to speak with a subject-matter expert.

    Choose the questions most relevant to the expert's experience and your business idea.

    Understanding the industry

    • Can you tell me about your experience in this field?
    • What are some of the biggest challenges facing this industry?
    • How have these challenges changed over time?
    • What trends or developments do you think are important to understand?

    Exploring the problem

    • How is [SPECIFIC PROBLEM OR PROCESS] typically handled today?
    • What makes this problem difficult to address?
    • Which organizations or groups are most affected?
    • What approaches have you seen work well or poorly?
    • Can you share an example from your experience?

    Understanding constraints

    • What technical requirements should someone entering this market understand?
    • Are there regulations, standards, or certifications that may apply?
    • What barriers make it difficult for new solutions to enter the market?
    • What common mistakes do new businesses make in this area?

    Exploring opportunities

    • Where do you see gaps or unmet needs in the market?
    • Are there changes that could create new opportunities?
    • What existing solutions or organizations should I investigate?
    • What assumptions about this market would you encourage me to question?

    Closing the conversation

    • What important questions haven't I asked?
    • Are there resources you recommend reviewing?
    • Is there someone else whose perspective would be valuable?
    • Would you be open to a follow-up question if something comes up during my research?

    You don't need to cover every topic in one interview.

    Focus on the expert's area of knowledge and ask follow-up questions when something deserves further exploration.

    SME interview notes template

    Use a consistent format to document your conversations.

    Category Notes
    Interview date
    Expert's field or specialization
    Main questions investigated
    Important industry insights
    Technical or regulatory considerations
    Existing solutions or approaches
    Risks or barriers identified
    Potential opportunities
    Recommended resources or contacts
    Assumptions supported or challenged
    Questions requiring further research

    Keep the information organized so you can compare insights across different interviews.

    Avoid collecting or sharing confidential information without appropriate permission.

    Assumption and evidence tracker

    SME interviews can help you identify assumptions that need further investigation.

    Create a simple table to track what you've learned.

    Assumption Expert insight Supporting evidence Next step
    Assumption A
    Assumption B
    Assumption C

    For each assumption, consider:

    • Did the expert support or challenge it?
    • Was their response based on direct experience, research, or professional opinion?
    • Are there credible sources that support the insight?
    • Should you investigate it with additional experts or potential customers?

    This helps you avoid treating one person's opinion as a confirmed fact.

    Tech support

    LinkedIn

    LinkedIn can help you identify professionals with experience in your target industry or area of research.

    Search for people by role, industry, organization, or area of specialization.

    Review their backgrounds before reaching out and explain clearly why you'd value their perspective.

    Google Scholar

    Google Scholar is a search tool for academic publications and research.

    You can use it to find relevant studies, identify researchers working in your field, and explore technical or scientific information related to your idea.

    When reviewing research, consider its publication date, methodology, and relevance to your specific question.

    Calendly

    Calendly is a scheduling platform that can help you coordinate interviews with experts.

    Sharing your availability can make it easier to arrange conversations with busy professionals.

    Google Meet

    Google Meet is a video meeting platform you can use to conduct remote SME interviews.

    It can help you connect with experts outside your immediate geographic area.

    If you plan to record an interview, obtain the participant's permission and review the available recording and privacy settings.

    Google Docs

    Google Docs can help you prepare interview questions, organize notes, and summarize findings.

    Consider keeping a separate document for each interview and maintaining a shared summary of important insights.

    Store your notes securely and limit access when they contain confidential or sensitive information.

    Put it into practice

    Optional but highly recommended: Conduct one or two SME interviews to strengthen your understanding of the problem, industry, or market you're exploring.

    You don't need to complete this exercise before continuing with customer discovery. Consider it an additional opportunity to challenge assumptions and identify information you may have overlooked.

    Start by identifying an important knowledge gap.

    For example:

    • I don't fully understand how this industry operates.
    • I'm unsure which technical requirements might apply.
    • I need to learn more about relevant regulations.
    • I want to understand why existing solutions work the way they do.
    • I need an experienced perspective on a problem I've identified.

    Then:

    1. Identify: Find one or two people with relevant expertise.
    2. Prepare: Research their background and develop a short list of questions.
    3. Connect: Reach out with a clear explanation of what you're researching and why their experience would be valuable.
    4. Interview: Ask open-ended questions, listen carefully, and explore specific examples.
    5. Document: Record important insights, assumptions, and recommended resources.
    6. Reflect: Decide what you've learned and what needs further investigation.

    After your conversations, ask yourself:

    • What did I learn that I didn't know before?
    • Did the experts challenge any of my assumptions?
    • What industry, technical, or regulatory considerations should I investigate further?
    • Were there differences between the experts' perspectives?
    • What information still needs to be verified?
    • How might these insights influence my customer discovery interviews?

    Summarize the most useful findings and identify any next steps.

    Consider sharing your insights with your TechAlliance advisor to discuss how they relate to your business idea and customer research.

    Remember: Expert interviews can strengthen your understanding of the market, but they don't replace speaking directly with potential customers.

    Use both perspectives to develop a clearer picture of the problem you're exploring.
